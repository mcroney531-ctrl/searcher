HMAC-signed, retried on failure, auto-disabled when broken. Webhooks push job lifecycle events to your endpoint in near-real-time instead of forcing you to poll. Available on Starter, Growth, Professional, and Custom plans, with these per-plan caps on active subscriptions: Plan Active webhook subscriptions Starter 1 Growth 3 Professional 5 Custom agreed per contract Why webhooks Polling the Pull API works well for batch refresh, but it's wasteful when you only want to react to changes. Webhooks let you stay event-driven: job.created — a job we haven't seen before is published job.updated — a tracked job changed (title, salary, status, etc.) job.closed — a job's listing stopped being live job.deleted — a job was removed (your store should delete it too) Webhook deliveries are at-least-once. Your handler must be idempotent — see Dedupe below. Quickstart Open Webhooks in your dashboard and click New Subscription . Give it a name, paste your HTTPS endpoint, pick the event types and an optional filter, and submit. Copy the signing secret on the one-time reveal screen — Jobven never shows it again. Deploy your endpoint with the verifier recipe below. Click Send test — either from the create-success modal or the subscription detail page — to fire a deterministic webhook.test event at your endpoint. The subscription stays in pending_verification until your endpoint returns 2xx , at which point it flips to active and starts receiving real job events. New subscriptions start in pending_verification and do not receive any real job events until a Send test succeeds. This prevents an empty or broken endpoint from being flooded the moment you save. Subscription lifecycle Status Meaning How to leave it pending_verification Created (or freshly re-enabled). Not in the dispatch pool — no events are delivered. Send a test event from the dashboard. A 2xx response flips it to active automatically. active Receiving live events that match the filter, plus every job.deleted . Disable from the dashboard, or hit the failure thresholds (auto-disable). disabled_by_user You paused it. No events are delivered. Re-enable from the dashboard — this re-routes through pending_verification , so you'll need to send a fresh test first. disabled_due_to_failures Auto-disabled after 50 consecutive failures or 24h with no successful delivery. Hit Re-enable on the detail page. You'll get the postedAfter / postedBefore bounds for the gap so you can replay it through the Pull API, and the subscription returns to pending_verification until you send another successful test. The verification gate intentionally applies to every state transition that resumes delivery, including re-enable. The endpoint may have changed between when you paused and when you resumed — re-testing before flipping back to active means a broken endpoint can't silently start consuming quota on retries. A failed test event leaves the status untouched (still pending or disabled). Test failures do not count toward the auto-disable thresholds and do not bump the streak counters. Request shape Every delivery is a POST to your URL with these headers: POST /your/webhook/path HTTP/1.1 Content-Type: application/json User-Agent: Jobven-Webhook/1.0 Jobven-Event-Id: 8e3f5c44-1cd1-4a40-bb4f-9d4f95c54c1d Jobven-Event-Type: job.created Jobven-Subscription-Id: 7c2f8f7a-d8ab-4d4a-9a09-71b4e6dffb8a Jobven-Delivery-Attempt: 1 Jobven-Signature: t=1715520000,v1=2e3... The body is a JSON envelope: { " id " : " 8e3f5c44-1cd1-4a40-bb4f-9d4f95c54c1d " , " type " : " job.created " , " createdAt " : 1715520000 , " subscriptionId " : " 7c2f8f7a-d8ab-4d4a-9a09-71b4e6dffb8a " , " deliveryAttempt " : 1 , " data " : { " job " : { /* … see /docs/reference/jobs for the full shape */ } } } See Event reference for the payload shape of each event type. Signature verification Always verify the signature before trusting a payload. Compute HMAC-SHA256(t + "." + raw_body) with your subscription's signing secret and compare to the v1= value in Jobven-Signature . Reject if the timestamp drifts more than 300 seconds (replay protection). JSON parsing changes whitespace and breaks verification. Dedupe on Jobven-Event-Id Webhook deliveries are at-least-once. Persist the Jobven-Event-Id for each event you've processed and skip duplicates. The header is the same UUID across all retry attempts for the same event. Retries and timeouts A delivery is considered successful when your endpoint returns a 2xx status within 10 seconds . Otherwise we retry on the following schedule: Attempt Delay from previous 2 30 seconds 3 2 minutes 4 10 minutes 5 30 minutes After the fifth failed attempt we give up. We retry on 5xx , network timeouts, and 429 . Other 4xx responses ( 400 , 404 , 410 , 422 ) are treated as terminal — fix your endpoint, don't ignore them. Auto-disable, re-enable, and backfill To protect both sides, Jobven auto-disables a subscription that hits 50 consecutive failed deliveries or goes 24 hours with no successful delivery . The dashboard surfaces a banner and a re-enable flow. When you re-enable, Jobven returns the postedAfter / postedBefore bounds for the disable window so you can replay the gap through your existing Pull integration — see the Jobs API reference for the parameters. The subscription itself returns to pending_verification — send another successful test, and the live webhook stream resumes from that moment. Pull-backfill plus a successful test together cover the entire gap with no replay of stored events. Quota accounting Your plan's monthly allowance is measured in jobs delivered , shared between the Pull API and webhooks. A successful job.created or job.updated delivery counts one job, because it carries a full job record. There is no daily quota. job.closed and job.deleted are free , and they keep arriving even when your account is over its limit, so the jobs you already hold do not go stale. Test events do not count. Failed delivery attempts do not count — only successful ones. When you are over your limit, new and updated jobs are dropped, not queued , and are not re-sent when the period resets. Backfill the gap through the Pull API with postedAfter . Running out of quota Once you reach your monthly cap, Pull API requests return 429 and job.created and job.updated events are dropped rather than queued . job.closed and job.deleted are free and keep arriving, so the jobs you already hold stay current. This is not a pause. A dropped event is not stored and is not re-sent when your quota resets or when you upgrade, so any job that is created or updated while you are over the cap will never arrive as a webhook. Unlike auto-disable , there is no backfill window returned for it. If you go over, treat the gap the same way you would a disable window: replay it through the Pull API using postedAfter / postedBefore once your quota is available again. See the Jobs API reference for the parameters. We email you at 80%, 90%, and 100% of your cap. The 80% and 90% warnings are the ones worth acting on: by the time the 100% email arrives, events are already being dropped.A 429 response carries Retry-After and X-RateLimit-Reset headers, and a resetAt timestamp in the body, telling you when your quota next resets. Filter scope Subscriptions accept a structured filter that mirrors a subset of the public Pull API. Allowed keys: remoteType , experienceLevel , employmentType , industry , language , country , state , city , minSalary , maxSalary , hasVisaSponsorship , hasRelocation . See Jobs API reference for the semantics of each filter parameter. An empty filter delivers every active job event — it works, but it burns quota aggressively. Always narrow. job.deleted ignores your filter. You receive it whenever a job is removed, even if that job never matched.A deletion is owed to anyone who might be holding the record, and we can't work out who that is from the job's final state — a job can reach you under one set of attributes and be deleted under another. Sending it to everyone is the only way to guarantee nobody is left serving a job that no longer exists. If you don't recognise the id or applyUrl , ignore the event; there's nothing to clean up.The other three event types are filtered as described above.
